# Chapter 19. The Price of a Quantifier

<!-- cite:begin -->
From *On the Collatz Conjecture: Martingales, Momentum and Continued Fractions* by Nick Polson and Vadim Sokolov (2026), chapter 19. Cite as Polson and Sokolov (2026), *On the Collatz Conjecture: Martingales, Momentum and Continued Fractions*, edition 1, https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.24113412. Book page: https://vsokolov.org/books/collatz/. Source: https://github.com/VadimSokolov/collatz.
<!-- cite:end -->

This chapter reads a seventeen state, two symbol Turing machine that beeps infinitely often if and only if the Collatz conjecture is true (Theorem 19.1, from the Mira Cult repository, 2026) as a statement about the logical shape of the problem. The conjecture is a Pi_2 sentence of arithmetic and the weak conjecture, no nontrivial cycles, is Pi_1; the difference is one quantifier, which costs three states over Reams's fourteen state weak Collatz machine, a heavy tail in the running time, and an a priori bound on the stopping time in any proof in Peano arithmetic. Proved: a computable bound S on the total stopping time turns the conjecture into a Pi_1 sentence, equivalent to the non halting of an ordinary Turing machine and to the unsolvability of one Diophantine equation (Theorem 19.2). The machine's clock is quadratic in the value, and in the Bernoulli model with factors 1/2 and 3/2 the clock scaled by n squared has a tail of index one half, infinite mean and a tight family (Theorem 19.3, by optional stopping and a change of measure, with the Kesten and Goldie perpetuity as a remark); no function above half the square of the value is a supermartingale (Proposition 19.5). Computed: records of the total stopping time to 10^6, an empirical tail index of 0.513 for the deterministic clock, and an independent simulator that agrees with the macro rules in 96 000 configurations and in 2 times 10^8 transitions from the blank tape. The open problems Q1 to Q5 ask for minimality of seventeen states, a halting machine under a stopping time bound, constants for the clock, deterministic excursion densities and binary encodings.

## From the literature

- the seventeen state beeping machine equivalent to the full Collatz conjecture, with its macro rules and closed form running times (Mira Cult repository, 2026; listed on the Busy Beaver wiki as not independently verified, and checked by simulation in Section 19.6 of the chapter)
- the weak Collatz conjecture in BB(14) (Reams 2026, verified by Ligocki), Collatz like rules in small Busy Beaver machines (Michel 1993, Aaronson 2020), and the cryptid list of the Busy Beaver wiki
- the MRDP theorem (Matiyasevich 1993), Kreisel's theorem on provably total functions in the form of Fairtlough and Wainer (1998), and Conway's undecidability of generalized Collatz maps (1972)
- the branching random walk prediction of the stopping time constant 41.6776 (Lagarias and Weiss 1992)
- the cycle identity and its Diophantine bounds (Steiner 1977, Simons and de Weger 2005)
- the implicit renewal theorem for perpetuities (Kesten 1973, Goldie 1991), used in a remark of the chapter

## New results

<!-- results:begin -->
- [A computable bound on the total stopping time makes the Collatz conjecture a Pi_1 sentence](../RESULTS.md#collapse_of_a_computable_stopping_time_bound) (Theorem 19.2, page 327; proved): Assuming a computable bound S on the total stopping time turns the Collatz conjecture into a Pi_1 sentence, equivalent to the non halting of an ordinary Turing machine and to the unsolvability of one Diophantine equation, and the sentence implies the conjecture. New in this book.
- [Tail of the quadratic clock in the Bernoulli model: index one half, infinite mean, tight](../RESULTS.md#tail_of_the_clock_in_the_bernoulli_model) (Theorem 19.3, page 330; proved): In the Bernoulli model the quadratic clock of the machine, scaled by n squared, has a tail of index one half with explicit lower constant, so its mean is infinite for every n at least 2 while the scaled family is tight. New in this book.
- [No supermartingale majorizes half the square in the Bernoulli model](../RESULTS.md#no_supermartingale_majorant_of_the_clock) (Proposition 19.5, page 332; proved): In the Bernoulli model no nonnegative function above half the square of the value is a supermartingale from some start, so a Lyapunov function bounding the clock's per step cost cannot decrease in expectation. New in this book.
- [Problem Q1: is seventeen states minimal for a beeping machine equivalent to the full Collatz conjecture](../RESULTS.md#minimality_of_seventeen_states_problem) (Problem Q1, page 333; open): Decide whether seventeen states is minimal for a beeping machine equivalent to the full Collatz conjecture; the authors conjecture that the three state difference from the weak conjecture is close to the price of the Pi_2 quantifier in this encoding. New in this book.
- [Problem Q2: a halting machine under the stopping time conjecture](../RESULTS.md#halting_machine_under_the_stopping_time_conjecture_problem) (Problem Q2, page 333; open): Give an explicit formally proved BB(m) machine that halts exactly when the stopping time bound with a logarithmic budget fails, by adding a budget to the arithmetic core. New in this book.
- [Problem Q3: explicit constants and the deterministic tail of the clock](../RESULTS.md#explicit_constants_for_the_clock_problem) (Problem Q3, page 333; open): Give explicit constants in the tail theorem and the perpetuity constant, and decide whether the deterministic clock has the same tail and whether the empirical index 0.513 converges to one half. New in this book.
- [Problem Q4: a deterministic density version of the excursion bound](../RESULTS.md#deterministic_excursion_density_problem) (Problem Q4, page 333; open): Prove that the set of n up to N whose maximal excursion is at least K n has size at most C N over K, and decide whether it follows from the methods of the backward tree chapter. New in this book.
- [Problem Q5: the state count at which the clock turns logarithmic](../RESULTS.md#binary_encodings_problem) (Problem Q5, page 333; open): Find the state count at which a binary encoding changes the clock from quadratic to logarithmic per step, and decide whether the tail of the clock then becomes light. New in this book.
