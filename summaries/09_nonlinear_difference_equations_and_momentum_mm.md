# Chapter 9. Nonlinear Difference Equations and Momentum MM

This chapter studies the nonlinear difference equations behind both halves of the book: the Collatz recursion as a switched system, and Switching MM as a momentum MM scheme. For Collatz, the proportion of odd steps decides whether an orbit grows, with threshold 1/log2 3, and a simplified version of the Krasikov difference inequality method on the inverse tree certifies growth exponents up to 0.477 (residues modulo 3^7) for the integers whose orbit passes through a given number not divisible by 3 and not on a cycle, below the 0.84 of Krasikov and Lagarias. For optimization, Switching MM with step one over L decreases the objective on alternate iterates for every L smooth function. The joint spectral radius of its descent and injection matrices is computed exactly because the descent matrix has rank one; a transient bound is stated as a conjecture, and conditionally on it every switching rule is exponentially stable on quadratics. For mu strongly convex, L smooth functions, two steps contract the distance to the minimizer by an explicit factor, which is the exact worst case, confirmed by performance estimation (PEP). A modified equation shows that Switching MM has no inertial limit: its continuous time limit is a first order gradient flow with implicit gradient regularization.

## From the literature

The difference inequality method for the inverse Collatz tree (Krasikov 1989), improved to exponents 0.81 (Applegate and Lagarias 1995) and 0.84 (Krasikov and Lagarias 2003); switched systems (Liberzon 2003); the joint spectral radius (Rota and Strang 1960; Jungers 2009) and its rank one reduction to a maximum cycle mean problem (Ahmadi and Parrilo 2012); the MM reading of gradient descent (Lange, Hunter and Yang 2000); the Lyapunov function of Nesterov's method (Nesterov 2004; Wilson, Recht and Jordan 2021); performance estimation (Drori and Teboulle 2014; Taylor, Hendrickx and Glineur 2017) and the function value contraction of a gradient step (Taylor, Hendrickx and Glineur 2018); modified equations (Hairer, Lubich and Wanner 2006) and implicit gradient regularization (Barrett and Dherin 2021).

## New results

<!-- results:begin -->
- [Certified growth exponents for the inverse Collatz tree from a simplified difference inequality method](../RESULTS.md#certified_growth_exponents_for_the_inverse_collatz_tree) (Theorem 9.3, page 158; proved): A simplified version of the Krasikov difference inequality method turns lower bounds on how many integers reach a given number into a finite supersolution test over residues modulo a power of 3, and certifies exponents up to 0.477, far below the best known 0.84. New in this book.
- [Counting bound with exponent 0.477 for integers reaching 1](../RESULTS.md#simplified_counting_bound_for_integers_reaching_one) (Corollary 9.4, page 159; proved): The simplified method certifies, with a vector found by computer and checked in exact integer arithmetic, that at least x to the power 0.477 integers up to x reach 1, below the exponent 0.84 of Krasikov and Lagarias (2003). New in this book.
- [Switching MM decreases the objective on alternate iterates for every smooth function](../RESULTS.md#smm_monotone_on_alternate_iterates) (Proposition 9.5, page 161; proved): Switching MM with step one over L decreases the objective on alternate iterates for every L smooth function, convex or not, with an explicit decrease that yields a bound on the smallest gradient norm. New in this book.
- [Exact joint spectral radius of the descent and injection matrices of Switching MM](../RESULTS.md#smm_exact_joint_spectral_radius) (Proposition 9.6, page 162; proved): Because the descent matrix has rank one, the joint spectral radius of the descent and injection matrices of Switching MM is the larger of the spectral radius of the injection matrix and a supremum over a scalar sequence, a variant of a rank one device of Ahmadi and Parrilo (2012). New in this book.
- [Transient bound conjecture for the injection matrix of Switching MM](../RESULTS.md#smm_transient_bound_conjecture) (Conjecture 9.7, page 163; open): Conjecture that the transient of repeated injection steps of Switching MM never exceeds the first descent factor, supported by computation on a grid of 200 momentum values and 300 normalized steps. New in this book.
- [Every switching rule between descent and injection is stable on quadratics, conditional on the transient bound](../RESULTS.md#every_switching_rule_is_stable_on_quadratics) (Corollary 9.8, page 163; conditional on the transient bound $|\tau_j|<1$ for every $j\ge0$ at the fixed normalized step $a$, where $\tau_j=u^\top N^jv$ is defined in the statement; it is the content of the transient bound conjecture ($|\tau_j|\le1-a$ for all $a\in(0,1]$, $\beta\in[0,1)$ and $j\ge0$), which has been checked numerically on a finite grid of parameters and is not proved): If the transient bound holds, every switching rule between descent and injection steps of Switching MM, including adaptive and state dependent rules, is exponentially stable on a quadratic at step one over L. New in this book.
- [Exact linear rate of Switching MM on smooth strongly convex functions](../RESULTS.md#smm_linear_rate_on_smooth_strongly_convex_functions) (Theorem 9.9, page 164; proved): For strongly convex and L smooth functions, two steps of Switching MM at step one over L contract the distance to the minimizer by the product of the classical one step factors of a long and a short gradient step, which explicit functions show is the exact worst case over the class, and the function gap by its square, using the one step function value bound of Taylor, Hendrickx and Glineur (2018). New in this book.
- [Speed of Switching MM relative to gradient descent on smooth strongly convex functions](../RESULTS.md#smm_speed_relative_to_gradient_descent) (Corollary 9.10, page 165; proved): For condition numbers above an explicit threshold, the per step rate of Switching MM on the whole class of smooth strongly convex functions has normalized limit two plus beta over two, the slow mode step multiplier of the quadratic analysis, against one for gradient descent. New in this book.
- [Modified equation for period two gradient steps](../RESULTS.md#modified_equation_for_period_two_steps) (Proposition 9.11, page 165; proved): To second order, two gradient steps of lengths h1 and h2 follow the gradient flow of the objective plus a multiple of its squared gradient norm, with coefficient one quarter of the mean square step over the mean step; the single step case is the implicit gradient regularization of Barrett and Dherin (2021). New in this book.
