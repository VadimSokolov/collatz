Which questions does the book leave open, and which finite computations are worth doing? The chapter restates the open problems of the book in their sharpest form, for the divergence half of the Collatz conjecture (O1 Cesàro equidistribution, O2 trajectory mixing modulo powers of 3, O3 and O3' certificates with growing horizon and potentials of unbounded depth, O4 the third cumulant of total stopping times and the variance deficit, O5 uniform integrability, which is settled), for the cycle half (C1 an arithmetic sharpening of the cycle identity, C2 effective irrationality measures for log_2 3, C3 sharper accountings at the next rungs of the ladder), and for Switching MM (S1 rates beyond quadratics and S3 the best period two ratio, both partly settled, S2 stochastic analysis, S4 adaptive switching). It says what is known for each and flags formulations that turn out to be wrong. It then lists nine computational projects, P1 to P9, each with a success criterion: a machine checked cycle bound in Lean 4, thresholds and the window lemma, orbits from very large starting values, total stopping time cumulants at scale, Switching MM against Nesterov, heavy ball and Adam, periodic schedules, Erdős 125 beyond 4^13, perfect samplers beyond the coefficient stopping time, and trees of slow integers. The unnumbered Gallery of Orbits that follows shows seven computed plates.

## From the literature

Questions going back to Terras (1976), Steiner (1978) and Eliahou (1993), restated as O1, O3, C2 and the cycle formulation; the fine scale mixing results of Tao (2022) behind O2; the explicit irrationality measure of log_2 3 of Rhin (1987); the cycle length record of Hercher (2023) and the verification to 2^71 of Bařina (2025); the note of Polson and Sokolov (2026) on the AlphaProof Nexus system of Tsoukalas and others (2026), the source of C1 and of the Erdős 125 analogy; in the gallery, the density bound of Hasler and Melfi (2024) and the branching random walk model of Lagarias and Weiss (1992).

## New results

This chapter poses problems and projects and proves no theorem of its own.

<!-- results:begin -->
- [Trajectory mixing of Syracuse residues modulo powers of 3](../RESULTS.md#trajectory_mixing_of_syracuse_residues) (Problem O2, page 255; open): Asks for a proof that, from a random starting value, the residues modulo a power of 3 along a Syracuse orbit are distributed as in the random model of the book, whose Markov chain mixes exactly; numerically, modulo 243, they approach the model law at the rate of independent sampling. New in this book.
- [Failure fraction of Lyapunov potentials of growing depth for the Syracuse map](../RESULTS.md#failure_fraction_of_potentials_of_growing_depth) (Problem O3$'$, page 256; open): Asks how small the fraction of odd integers at which a Lyapunov potential fails can be made when the potential may read further ahead along the orbit, and whether it can be driven to zero; the answer is a question about the random model of the book, not about the arithmetic of the map. New in this book.
- [Boundedness of the variance deficit and the third cumulant of total stopping times](../RESULTS.md#bounded_variance_deficit_of_total_stopping_times) (Problem O4, page 256; open): Asks for a proof that the shortfall of the variance of total stopping times against the random model, which an exact census finds to be an additive and apparently bounded term concentrated in the final descent of the orbit, stays bounded as the starting values grow, and for an explanation of their skewness, which is smaller than the model predicts. New in this book.
- [Arithmetic sharpening of the window bound on the least element of a Collatz cycle](../RESULTS.md#arithmetic_sharpening_of_the_cycle_window) (Problem C1, page 257; open): Asks for an improvement, drawn from the arithmetic of the parity vector, of the bound on the least element of a hypothetical Collatz cycle; a strong enough improvement would reduce the cycle half of the conjecture to a finite computation. First stated in Polson and Sokolov (2026), Five Bayesian problems raised by AlphaProof Nexus, manuscript.
- [Verification ranges that exclude the later rungs under Hercher's sharper accounting](../RESULTS.md#verification_ranges_for_later_rungs_under_hercher_accounting) (Problem C3, page 258; open): Asks for the computation that would extend Hercher's computer assisted exclusion of Collatz cycles of a given length to the next lengths in line, the rungs of the one sided continued fraction ladder of log2 3. New in this book.
- [Exact worst case function gap rate of Switching MM for small condition numbers](../RESULTS.md#exact_function_gap_rate_of_switching_mm_for_small_condition_numbers) (Problem S1, page 258; open): The worst case rate of Switching MM is known exactly for the distance to the minimizer, and for the function gap on ill conditioned problems; the problem asks for the exact function gap rate, and the function that attains it, on well conditioned problems, where numerical performance estimation shows that the known bound is not tight. New in this book.
- [Five thirds noise bound of Switching MM beyond quadratics](../RESULTS.md#five_thirds_noise_bound_of_switching_mm_beyond_quadratics) (Problem S2, page 258; open): Asks to extend the proven robustness of Switching MM to gradient noise, a stationary error at most five thirds of that of gradient descent for every momentum coefficient below one, from quadratic objectives to all smooth strongly convex objectives. New in this book.
- [A principled adaptive switching rule for Switching MM](../RESULTS.md#adaptive_switching_rule_for_switching_mm) (Problem S4, page 259; open): Asks for a rule, using only the smoothness constant and observed function values, that decides when Switching MM injects momentum, keeping its proven robustness to gradient noise while matching the speed of momentum applied at every step on noiseless problems. New in this book.
