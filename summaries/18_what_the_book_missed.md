# Chapter 18. What the Book Missed

From *On the Collatz Conjecture: Martingales, Momentum and Continued Fractions* by Nick Polson and Vadim Sokolov (2026), chapter 18. Book page: https://vsokolov.org/books/collatz/. Source: https://github.com/VadimSokolov/collatz.

This chapter asks what the book left unproved within reach on the two halves of the Collatz conjecture, cycles and divergence. For cycles it sharpens the basic inequality on the smallest element of a cycle with r odd elements: the exact cycle identity 3x_0(2^{delta_c}-1) = sum of 2^{W_t} over the walk of halvings (Theorem 18.2) gives the minimum inequality with constant 1/(2 log 2) = 0.7213 in place of 1 (Theorem 18.3, Corollary 18.4), and the positive rational cycles of the Christoffel words attain it (Theorem 18.5), so no inequality in r, s and the minimum alone can do better. A matching bound holds for the largest element (Theorem 18.6, Corollary 18.7). At the record rung r = 72 057 431 991 a rational cycle has minimum above 2^71.41, so excluding integer cycles needs an arithmetic gain of at least 1.33 (Corollary 18.8), and the word of a cycle above 2^71 must stay in a narrow band around the line t log_2 3 (Proposition 18.9), with exact word counts by dynamic programming. For divergence it recasts the Cesàro criterion as a statement about empirical measures on the 2 adic integers (Theorem 18.10) and truncates it to a finite modulus: an orbit is bounded if the odd iterates in classes 1 and 5 modulo 8, with class 5 counted twice, have lower frequency above log_2(3/2) = 0.585 (Proposition 18.11). It also proves an exponential tail in the stride model (Proposition 18.13) and corrects the long run variance on the T_0 clock to (2/27)(log 6)^2 (Proposition 18.14). The open problems M1 to M5 are: records of the sharp ladder bound, branch and bound at q_23, an inequality for integer cycles, counting words with large Phi, and the limsup version of the modulus criterion. Nothing here proves or disproves the conjecture.

## From the literature

- the minimum inequality with constant 1 (Crandall 1978, Eliahou 1993) and Hercher's Theorem 27 with constant 3/4 (Hercher 2023)
- every periodic parity word is the word of a rational cycle, and the frequency bound for divergent trajectories (Lagarias 1985), whose heuristic count of cycles the chapter sharpens
- balanced words and the study of m cycles (Steiner 1977, Simons and de Weger 2005)
- Koksma's inequality and Ostrowski's discrepancy bound (Kuipers and Niederreiter 1974)
- persistence asymptotics for random walks (Iglehart 1974, Doney 1989), used for the density of large coefficient stopping times (Proposition 18.12, an application of these theorems with a numerical constant, not catalogued)

## New results

<!-- results:begin -->
- [The walk of halvings seen from the smallest and the largest element of a cycle](../RESULTS.md#walk_of_halvings_seen_from_the_extremes) (Lemma 18.1, page 300; proved): Read from the smallest element of a cycle, the halving count never gets ahead of the line t log_2 3 by more than the closing defect, and read from the largest element it never falls behind that line. New in this book.
- [Exact form of the cycle identity for positive rational cycles](../RESULTS.md#exact_form_of_the_cycle_identity) (Theorem 18.2, page 301; proved): For every positive rational cycle and every starting element, three times the element times (2 to the closing defect, minus one) equals the sum of 2 to the walk of halvings over one period, the walk being read from that element. New in this book.
- [Sharp minimum inequality for cycles of the Syracuse map](../RESULTS.md#sharp_minimum_inequality) (Theorem 18.3, page 301; proved): The smallest element of a cycle is below a bound that depends only on the number of odd elements and the closing defect, with constant 1/(2 log 2) in place of 1, uniformly in the word. New in this book.
- [The sharp minimum bound on the ladder of rungs and its asymptotics](../RESULTS.md#minimum_inequality_on_the_ladder) (Corollary 18.4, page 301; proved): At a rung of the continued fraction ladder of log_2 3 every cycle has its smallest element below G(r), and the sum defining G(r) is r/(2 log 2) up to a lower order term controlled by the partial quotients. New in this book.
- [Christoffel cycles attain the sharp minimum inequality](../RESULTS.md#christoffel_cycles_are_extremal) (Theorem 18.5, page 302; proved): The positive rational cycle of the lower Christoffel word of slope s/r has 3 M_w Lambda between 2 to the minus 2 delta times beta_r = r/(2 log 2) + 1/4 + O(1/r) and the sharp bound B_r(delta), and along the rungs the ratio to the sharp bound tends to 1, so no inequality in r, s and the minimum alone can improve the constant 1/(2 log 2). New in this book.
- [Sharp maximum inequality for positive rational cycles](../RESULTS.md#sharp_maximum_inequality) (Theorem 18.6, page 303; proved): The largest element of a long cycle is at least about 1/log 2, or 1.4427, times the elementary bound r/(3 Lambda), and the Christoffel cycles attain this. New in this book.
- [Spread of the elements of a long cycle: a factor of about two](../RESULTS.md#spread_of_a_long_cycle) (Corollary 18.7, page 304; proved): The largest element of a long cycle at a rung is at least about twice the smallest, and the extremal cycles span exactly that factor. New in this book.
- [Arithmetic gap at the rung r star: a rational cycle above 2 to the 71.41](../RESULTS.md#arithmetic_gap_at_the_current_record_rung) (Corollary 18.8, page 305; computed): A positive rational cycle with 72 057 431 991 odd elements has smallest element above 2^71.41, so excluding integer cycles at that size with verification to 2^71 needs a property of integers that improves the sharp minimum inequality by a factor of at least 1.33; the numerical constants come from an exact interval computation of B_r. New in this book.
- [Band: the word of a long cycle stays near the line t log_2 3 for a positive fraction of indices](../RESULTS.md#band_for_the_word_of_a_cycle) (Proposition 18.9, page 306; proved): A cycle with large minimum has a positive fraction of indices at which the halving count stays within b of t log_2 3, and at a cycle with r star odd elements and minimum at least 2^71 that fraction is at least 47.6 percent for b = 3. New in this book.
- [Divergence as an invariant measure on the 2 adic integers (reformulation of Lagarias's frequency bound)](../RESULTS.md#divergence_as_an_invariant_measure) (Theorem 18.10, page 308; proved): An orbit of a positive integer is unbounded exactly when some, equivalently every, weak limit of its empirical measures gives the odd cylinder mass at least log_3 2; this repackages Lagarias's frequency bound as a statement about invariant measures. New in this book.
- [Finite modulus criterion: a lower frequency bound on classes modulo 2^m forces a bounded orbit](../RESULTS.md#finite_modulus_criterion_for_bounded_orbits) (Proposition 18.11, page 309; proved): An orbit is bounded as soon as the odd iterates in the classes 1 and 5 modulo 8, with class 5 counted twice, have lower frequency above log_2(3/2), a bound that equidistribution would exceed with 0.75. New in this book.
- [Exponential tail of the stopping time in the stride model](../RESULTS.md#exponential_tail_in_the_stride_model) (Proposition 18.13, page 311; proved): In the stride model with independent geometric halving exponents the probability of not having come down by stride n is at most x^p M(p)^n, a bound that decays exponentially at rate 0.0550 per stride at the best exponent. New in this book.
- [Long run variance of the log path on the T_0 clock, correcting the factor one third remark](../RESULTS.md#variance_on_the_t0_clock) (Proposition 18.14, page 311; proved): In the renewal model the long run variance per step of the log path on the clock of T_0 is (2/27)(log 6)^2, about 0.2378, and the dispersion of the total stopping time grows like 269.7 log n; this corrects an earlier remark of the book. New in this book.
- [Problem M1: records of the sharp ladder bound occur only at rungs](../RESULTS.md#records_of_the_ladder_problem) (Problem M1, page 313; open): Prove that the records of the sharp bound B_r(delta(r))/(3 log 2 delta(r)) occur only at the rungs of the ladder, as is proved for the elementary bound F. New in this book.
- [Problem M2: branch and bound exclusion of cycles at r = q_23](../RESULTS.md#branch_and_bound_at_q23_problem) (Problem M2, page 313; open): Implement a branch and bound search over cycle words read from the minimum and find the smallest verification range at which it excludes cycles with 137 528 045 312 odd elements. New in this book.
- [Problem M3: an inequality for integer cycles that fails for some rational cycle](../RESULTS.md#integer_cycle_inequality_problem) (Problem M3, page 313; open): Find, without computer search, an inequality valid for integer cycles but false for some rational cycle that improves the sharp minimum inequality by a constant factor at all large rungs. New in this book.
- [Problem M4: counting admissible words with large Phi](../RESULTS.md#words_with_large_phi_problem) (Problem M4, page 313; open): Count the admissible cycle words with Phi at least c times B_r, for c between 0.75 and 1, and prove that the exponential rate of this count exists and compute it. New in this book.
- [Problem M5: the limsup version of the finite modulus criterion](../RESULTS.md#limsup_version_problem) (Problem M5, page 313; open): Prove that along every Syracuse orbit the limsup of f_1 + 2 f_5 exceeds log_2(3/2), a weaker statement than the liminf criterion that would still imply that no orbit is unbounded. New in this book.
