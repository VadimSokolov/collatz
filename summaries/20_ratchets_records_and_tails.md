# Chapter 20. Ratchets, Records and Tails

This chapter, added after the first edition, applies the methods of the book to Conway's generalized Collatz maps and takes up Problems M1, M4, B1 and B2. Nothing in it proves the conjecture; it sharpens what a proof must do. On the subclass of Conway maps whose multipliers are coprime to the modulus, the digit bijection, the Lyapunov function, the martingale bound and density one descent hold with full proofs, while Conway's theorem and an unprovability proposition show that no method covers the whole class. The equipartition argument is the entropy dual of the Lyapunov argument and gives the same exponent, the entropy deficit 0.05004 bits per step. Two Conway maps with bounded orbits can alternate to a map with divergent orbits (a deterministic Parrondo pair); measure preservation forbids this for a common modulus, and with two moduli the Collatz map is one of two losing games that win together. For cycles, the sharp minimum inequality is an equality for the Christoffel cycle at every rung, Problem M1 is false as stated and true in its exact form (records occur only at rungs), and for Problem M4 there are exact counts, a rigorous Chernoff upper bound and a supermultiplicative lower bound. Granting Lau's theorem (an unrefereed preprint), the conjecture is equivalent to a polynomial bound on the tail of the orbit minimum, where Tao's theorem gives a logarithmic one. For Problem B2 a Feynman and Kac recursion computes single Fourier coefficients of the Syracuse variable to level 2000, and the largest decays like n^(-3/2) 3^(-0.05004 n), conjectured and not proved; the Switching MM viewpoint and an experiment on basins of 3x+d maps (one cycle in 312 455 captures 3.7 percent of starting values) close the chapter, followed by open problems R1 to R14. The chapter credits Halbeisen and Hungerbuhler (1997) with the exact minimum bound and the concatenation identity.

## From the literature

- Conway's theorem that multiplicative generalized Collatz maps compute every partial recursive function (Conway 1972)
- the theorem of Tao (2022) on almost bounded values in logarithmic density, and the positive density theorems of Mazur (2026) and Lau (2026), unrefereed, on which the tail criterion depends
- Parrondo's paradox of two losing games that win together, in its deterministic form here
- the largest minimal element of a rational cycle with given length and number of odd steps, with the concatenation identity (Halbeisen and Hungerbuhler 1997), of which the chapter's exact minimum bound is the same statement in other notation
- Christoffel words and the mechanical word of a given intercept; a recent preprint on Christoffel words as extremal structures in the same setting (2026), not compared line by line
- Sanov and Chernoff large deviation rates, and Cramer type moderate deviations

## New results

<!-- results:begin -->
- [Digit bijection for coprime Conway maps](../RESULTS.md#digit_bijection_of_coprime_conway_maps) (Theorem 20.4, page 337; proved): For a coprime Conway map the first k digits of x modulo d^k are a bijection onto digit strings, so on a complete residue system they are independent and uniform. New in this book.
- [Lyapunov and martingale structure of coprime Conway maps](../RESULTS.md#lyapunov_and_martingale_structure_of_coprime_conway_maps) (Theorem 20.5, page 338; proved): Under Haar measure the multiplier moments of a coprime Conway map give a family of martingales, drift negative exactly when the product of multipliers is below d^d, a maximal inequality with exponent theta star, and a uniform bound on the probability that the multiplier reaches 1. New in this book.
- [Density one descent for coprime Conway maps of negative drift](../RESULTS.md#density_one_descent_for_coprime_conway_maps) (Theorem 20.6, page 339; proved): For a coprime Conway map with negative drift the integers with an iterate smaller than themselves within k steps have lower density at least one minus rho_min to the k, so almost every integer descends. New in this book.
- [A Conway map whose descent no sound theory proves](../RESULTS.md#unprovable_descent_for_a_conway_map) (Proposition 20.7, page 340; proved): For any sound recursively axiomatized theory there is a Conway map all of whose orbits reach 1 but for which the theory does not prove this, so it has no Lyapunov function provable in the theory. New in this book.
- [Entropy and Lyapunov duality for coprime Conway maps](../RESULTS.md#entropy_and_lyapunov_duality) (Theorem 20.8, page 340; proved): The minimal relative entropy of digit laws with nonnegative log drift equals minus log base 2 of rho_min, and counts the digit strings of length k with multiplier at least 1 as 2 to the k times (log_2 d minus eta) up to o(k). New in this book.
- [A deterministic Parrondo pair of Conway maps](../RESULTS.md#deterministic_parrondo_pair) (Theorem 20.9, page 342; proved): Two modulus four Conway maps each of whose orbits reaches a cycle or fixed point have an alternation whose every orbit tends to infinity, with C(n)=9n+4 for even n. New in this book.
- [No ratchet under measure preservation](../RESULTS.md#no_ratchet_under_measure_preservation) (Theorem 20.10, page 343; proved): Under Haar measure the expected logarithmic multiplier of any composition of p copies of one coprime Conway map and q of another is p mu_A plus q mu_B, so two maps of negative drift cannot be combined into one of positive drift. New in this book.
- [Collatz as game A: a resonant pair with positive drift](../RESULTS.md#collatz_as_game_a_resonant_pair) (Theorem 20.11, page 344; proved): Both the Collatz map and a modulus three map lose, but their alternation has an invariant Haar measure, i.i.d. round multipliers and positive drift for every m at least 7, and almost every integer has a larger iterate. New in this book.
- [Exact bound on the smallest element of a rational cycle, attained by the Christoffel cycle](../RESULTS.md#exact_minimum_bound_for_rational_cycles) (Theorem 20.12, page 347; proved): Every positive rational cycle satisfies 3M(2^delta minus 1) at most B prime r of delta, with equality exactly for the mechanical word, and at a rung the maximal minimum over cycles is attained by the Christoffel cycle alone. The maximal minimum and its attainment by the mechanical word are due to Halbeisen and Hungerbuhler (1997); the characterization of equality for every closing defect is stated here. New in this book.
- [Concatenation identity for the first elements of rational cycles](../RESULTS.md#concatenation_of_rational_cycles) (Lemma 20.14, page 348; proved): For words with positive closing defects the first element of the concatenated cycle is a weighted mean of the first elements with explicit positive weights, and Phi is additive with a twist. Halbeisen and Hungerbuhler (1997) give the same identity. New in this book.
- [Additivity of the closing defect below the next rung](../RESULTS.md#additivity_of_defects_below_the_next_rung) (Lemma 20.15, page 348; proved): Between a rung q and the next rung the closing defect of r is the sum of those of q and r minus q, and partial sums of defects stay below 1. New in this book.
- [Records of the exact bound occur only at rungs](../RESULTS.md#records_of_the_exact_bound_occur_at_rungs) (Theorem 20.16, page 348; proved): For r not a rung the mechanical word is that of u followed by that of q, the exact bound at r is a weighted mean of those at u and q, and so a new record of the bound can occur only at a rung. New in this book.
- [The record statement M1 as stated fails](../RESULTS.md#statement_m1_as_stated_fails) (Proposition 20.17, page 349; proved): The ratio R(2q)/R(q) exceeds 1 for rungs q at least 2 with 2q below the next rung, so records of R occur at non rungs such as 10, 15, 82, 612 and 918. New in this book.
- [Chernoff bound at every rung for words with large Phi](../RESULTS.md#chernoff_upper_bound_at_every_rung) (Proposition 20.18, page 350; proved): The count of admissible words with Phi at least c B_r is bounded by a minimum over lambda of a matrix product partition function, with explicit nonnegative matrices indexed by depth. New in this book.
- [Supermultiplicativity of the counts of words with large Phi](../RESULTS.md#supermultiplicativity_of_the_word_counts) (Proposition 20.19, page 350; proved): Between rungs the count of words with large Phi satisfies N*(c,r) at least N*(c,u) N*(c,q), so the count at jq is at least the j th power of the count at q. New in this book.
- [Tail criterion: the conjecture is equivalent to a polynomial tail bound on the orbit minimum](../RESULTS.md#tail_criterion_for_the_collatz_conjecture) (Theorem 20.20, page 352; conditional on Lau's theorem (Theorem 13.1, from an unrefereed preprint), that for every t prime to 3 the integers whose orbit reaches t have lower natural density at least t^(-4)/D_A, which the book does not prove): Granting Lau's theorem, a least counterexample m star forces the tail E(C) of the orbit minimum to be at least 1/(16 m_star^4 D_A), so the conjecture is equivalent to a tail bound of that form for C at least 2^71. New in this book.
- [A polynomial tail bounds the least counterexample](../RESULTS.md#polynomial_tail_bounds_the_least_counterexample) (Corollary 20.21, page 352; conditional on Lau's theorem (Theorem 13.1, from an unrefereed preprint), that for every t prime to 3 the integers whose orbit reaches t have lower natural density at least t^(-4)/D_A, which the book does not prove): Granting Lau's theorem, a bound K C^(-4-epsilon) on the tail of the orbit minimum bounds any least counterexample explicitly, so the conjecture reduces to a finite computation. New in this book.
- [Cramer regime for the logarithmic multiplier of coprime Conway maps](../RESULTS.md#cramer_regime_for_coprime_conway_maps) (Proposition 20.25, page 354; proved): For a coprime Conway map with unequal multipliers and n uniform modulo d^k, the upper tail of the logarithmic multiplier has the Gaussian asymptotic with the Mills ratio factor for deviations x_k tending to infinity with x_k=o(k^(1/6)). New in this book.
- [Feynman and Kac recursion for the Fourier coefficients of the Syracuse variable](../RESULTS.md#feynman_kac_recursion_for_fourier_coefficients) (Proposition 20.26, page 354; proved): The Fourier coefficient u_n(B) of the Syracuse variable at the frequency 2^B satisfies an explicit recursion from level n-1 to level n, which with a truncated window of exponents computes the table to level n in O(n^2) operations. New in this book.
- [The Syracuse offset is Phi of the reversed word](../RESULTS.md#syracuse_variable_is_phi_of_the_reversed_word) (Proposition 20.28, page 356; proved): The offset of a word equals 3^(n-1) Phi of the reversed word over 2^(A_n), an integer over a power of two, so the Fourier phase at a power of two frequency is an ordinary real phase. New in this book.
- [Problem R1: Is there an undecidable problem inside the coprime class?](../RESULTS.md#undecidability_in_the_coprime_class_problem) (Problem R1, page 359; open): Decide whether the coprime class of Conway maps, which preserves Haar measure, contains an undecidable problem, equivalently whether measure preservation limits computational power.
