# Chapter 2. Parity Vectors and the Syracuse Map

This chapter isolates the parity test in the 3x+1 problem and asks what it determines for whole families of starting values. It fixes the standard, accelerated and Syracuse maps, defines the parity vector of an orbit, and proves the theorem of Terras and Everett: the parity vector of length k and the residue modulo 2^k determine each other, so a uniformly random residue gives k independent fair coin flips exactly. It then proves the affine form of k iterates, an explicit inverse of the parity vector map, the cycle formula, the exact Geometric law of the halving exponent v_2(3n+1) and the exact Syracuse drift log(3/4). In the 2 adic integers the Collatz map is conjugate to the Bernoulli shift, Haar measure is invariant, and the conjecture says that every positive integer has a parity vector ending in the tail 1010 and so on. The chapter then treats the stopping time and the coefficient stopping time of Terras with their exact counts, and proves the density theorem: integers with finite stopping time have natural density one, with the density of integers whose stopping time exceeds k at most e to the power minus Dk for a Kullback and Leibler divergence D of about 0.0347. All of this concerns ensembles of starting values, not a single orbit.

## From the literature

The bijection between parity vectors and residues modulo 2^k of Terras (1976) and Everett (1977); the density theorem of Terras (1976, 1979) and Everett (1977) with the exponential bound of Lagarias (1985); the coefficient stopping time, its exact counts and its conjecture, all of Terras (1976); the conjugacy to the shift of Lagarias (1985), with mixing as a case of a theorem of Matthews and Watts (1984); the explicit inverse of Bernstein (1994); the periodicity conjecture of Lagarias (1985, 1990); the affine identity, cycle formula and Geometric law, which are classical.
